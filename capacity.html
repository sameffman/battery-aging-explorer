Capacity fade

Discharge capacity at each periodic check-up, for the 228 cells of the aging experiment. Cells aged under the same condition share a color and line style (three cells per condition), so their spread shows cell-to-cell variation. Diamonds mark where a cell falls below 80% of its reference capacity, which is the cell's own first capacity by default. Capacity is shown in Ah for both references.

Notes on the data
  • Capacity is the dataset's estimate (cap_aged_est_Ah) from the discharge at each room-temperature check-up. The check-ups likely age the cells themselves: according to the dataset authors, each includes a 0–100–0% cycle for the capacity measurement and a 10–90–10% cycle to reach the impedance states of charge.
  • Check-up numbers run from CU 1 to CU 28, plus CU 29, a manual check-up performed at room temperature with air cooling. CU 29 is drawn but excluded from the 80% point, and marked “manual” in the tooltip.
  • 80% point: the first discharge capacity below 80% of a reference capacity, linearly interpolated between the two neighboring check-ups. Cells that never drop below it within the data have no marker.
  • 80% reference: by default each cell is measured against its own first discharge capacity (CU 1 for every cell). Each cell's threshold is then 80% of its own first capacity, so the thresholds differ slightly between cells, the chart has no single threshold line, and the diamonds sit at those heights. The alternative is the 3.0 Ah nominal capacity, a fixed 2.4 Ah line. It is the reference of the state-of-health scale in the dataset authors' paper, but the 80% threshold on it is this project's choice, not the authors'. Cells start slightly below 3.0 Ah, so against their own capacity they cross later, and a few cells reach 2.4 Ah without reaching 80% of their own first capacity. The dataset's own state-of-health column is not used.
  • Equivalent full cycles (EFC) are the mean of the total charged and discharged throughput divided by 3.0 Ah, as defined in the dataset readme. Calendar-aged cells only accumulate throughput during check-ups, so time is the meaningful axis for them.
  • Capacity recovery before CU 24: one of the three cells of each calendar condition above 10% SoC, the one with intermediate losses, was held at 10% SoC at its aging temperature for about 2.5 days just before CU 24. The nine of these cells that completed CU 24 regained part of their lost capacity, which appears as an upward step at CU 24, largest for cells aged at 90 and 100% SoC (for example P011-3: 2.667 Ah at CU 23, 2.688 Ah at CU 24). Most of the recovered capacity is lost again by CU 25.
  • Early shutoff: cells P012-1, P015-3 and P016-1 shut off at CU 24 and have no capacity after CU 23.
  • Voltage windows apply to cyclic aging and are the target charge and discharge voltages of the cycles; the authors relate them to state-of-charge ranges of approximately 0–100%, 10–100% and 10–90%.
  • A check-up with a missing discharge capacity is skipped, and the line connects the neighboring check-ups.

Data: M. Luh and T. Blank, Comprehensive battery aging dataset: capacity and impedance fade measurements of a lithium-ion NMC/C-SiO cell (version 2: result data), RADAR4KIT, 2024, doi:10.35097/1969, licensed CC BY 4.0. Values shown are derived from that dataset; see the project overview.
